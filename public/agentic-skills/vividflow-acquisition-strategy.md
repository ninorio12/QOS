# VividFlow Acquisition Strategy

## Core Principle

VividFlow acquisition is now generalist B2B for Swiss TPE/PME leaders, not real-estate-only. Every asset or outbound call must make the leader feel the operational cost of repetitive manual tasks, then point to VividFlow as the concrete IA-assisted way to free both the team and the dirigeant.

## Discovery Calls & Sales Training

When turning discovery calls into sales training, do **not** anchor the deliverable in the prospect's niche details. Extract the reusable VividFlow method:
- posture: consultant/operator, not AI vendor;
- philosophy: understand the operating system before proposing anything;
- questions: activity, team, tools, recurring friction, cost, urgency, ideal “super employee”;
- actions: reformulate, identify the first agentic wedge, prepare a concrete demo, book the next call.

Use client-specific details only as short annex/examples. Avoid overfitting the deck/training to sector words, niche metrics, or one prospect’s workflow. VividFlow must remain generalist and transposable.

## Required Inputs

Before writing copy, call scripts, pages, or visuals, define:
- ICP: Swiss TPE/PME leader, usually 45–60, practical and business-focused; do not default to real estate unless explicitly specified.
- Sector/activity: one plain-language activity label from the prospect’s website/list.
- Operational pain: one concrete workflow only.
- Business cost: time lost, forgotten follow-ups, slow response, client frustration, scattered information, team overload, direction pulled into details.
- Funnel stage: TOFU awareness, MOFU evaluation, BOFU conversion.
- CTA: current available offer, demo, custom page, short visio, audit if validated.

## ICP/content-led acquisition update

When Jonathan/Thomas are exploring a content-led acquisition system, do not force the default Swiss TPE/PME/outbound frame. Their current high-leverage ICP test is **entreprises d’accompagnement, conseil et transformation**: consultants, advisory firms, organismes de formation, structured business coaches, RH/recruitment/team coaching, digital transformation accompaniment, and other premium programs that sell expertise, follow-up, and transformation.

Position this as more precise than “agencies” and more serious than “coach Instagram”. Core angle:
- “Vous avez déjà une méthode. VividFlow construit le système IA qui la fait tourner, la vend mieux et la scale sans que tout dépende de vous.”
- “Les entreprises d’accompagnement ne manquent pas forcément de contenu. Elles manquent d’un système qui transforme leur expertise en exécution continue.”

For this ICP, acquisition should exploit VividFlow’s actual strengths: YouTube authority, Instagram frequency/desire, and a WhatsApp Channel as proximity/nurturing layer. See `references/whatsapp-channel-nurturing-system.md`.

### Dual acquisition engine rule

When Jonathan wants both content-led acquisition and telephonist outbound, separate the two machines:
- **Core content machine**: consultants, formateurs, accompagnants, agences conseil. Channels: YouTube authority, Instagram desire/frequency, WhatsApp Channel nurturing, DM setting.
- **Outbound telephonist machine**: one niche sprint at a time, opportunistic appointment booking for PME sectors. Do not brief the telephonist to “call any company”; give one weekly niche, one operational pain, one booking CTA, and CRM tagging.

Delivery can be generalist, but acquisition must feel niched. Never lead with “we accompany any company” when the call/email needs specificity.

### Current priority outbound niche: RH / recruitment

For a first focused outbound sprint, prioritize **cabinets RH / recrutement / chasse de tête B2B** over architects when speed to qualified meetings matters. Their pain is directly linked to revenue: lost follow-up can mean lost candidate, lost client, or lost placement. Use `references/recruitment-rh-outbound-campaign.md` for the full call/email structure.

## Angle Selection

Strong angles for general Swiss TPE/PME:
- “Your team spends too much time on small repetitive tasks instead of high-value work.”
- “Follow-ups, requests, and document prep should not depend on memory.”
- “The leader should keep control without being pulled into every operational detail.”
- “IA is useful when it removes concrete daily tasks, not when it stays a gadget.”
- “A custom page or short demo should show what would change in their actual company.”

If the sector is real estate, use the old real-estate angles only as examples, not as the default ICP:
- “Your leads are not missing. They are cooling down in manual follow-up.”
- “WhatsApp is a channel, not the command center of an agency.”
- “Reporting problems erode trust when information is scattered.”

Weak angles:
- Generic AI education.
- “The market is changing.”
- Productivity advice without business cost.
- Future-of-work philosophy.
- Overly polished consultant phrasing.

## Outbound téléphonique — ton VividFlow

When drafting or adapting cold/warm call scripts for Jonathan/Thomas, keep the old high-converting call energy:
- oral, direct, slightly imperfect French; not polished “AI assistant” language;
- use “Écoutez…”, “Je vais aller droit au but”, “Très bien, bah du coup ça fait sens que je vous appelle”; 
- create 2–3 easy yeses before pitching through simple confirmations, not discovery questions;
- do not ask “avez-vous cette problématique ?” when VividFlow already knows the pain; state the pain and ask if it speaks to them;
- avoid “Nous sommes une agence…” as the opening pitch; say “On a développé une IA qui permet…”;
- keep the prospect on the phone for the appointment when there is interest; the custom page/asset supports the close, it does not create an extra “go read then come back” step.

Generic phase-2 confirmations for Swiss TPE/PME:
- “Votre entreprise est bien basée à [Ville/Canton], c’est ça ?”
- “Vous êtes bien actif dans [secteur/activité principale], c’est ça ?”
- “Aujourd’hui l’objectif reste quand même de développer l’activité, j’imagine ?”
- “Si on peut vous montrer quelque chose de concret pour faire gagner du temps à votre équipe, vous êtes ouvert à regarder ?”

Pitch spine:
- “Très bien, bah du coup ça fait sens que je vous appelle.”
- “Je vais aller droit au but : on a développé une IA qui permet aux entreprises de réduire une grosse partie des tâches opérationnelles qui prennent du temps aux équipes et aux dirigeants.”
- Add 3–4 plain examples: demandes entrantes, suivis clients, relances, préparation d’informations.
- Ask: “C’est quelque chose qui vous parle ?”

## Conversion Spine

Use this sequence every time:
1. State the daily operational pain in plain words.
2. Show the business consequence for both team and leader.
3. Explain the IA mechanism without jargon.
4. Add proof/case studies only if verified; 2 short case studies are usually enough for first contact.
5. Give one CTA.

## Outbound consultatif — emails/pages personnalisés

When Jonathan asks for an email or custom page meant to motivate or impress a Swiss TPE/PME leader, do not write generic “AI can help” copy. Make the business case rational and concrete:
- Start from what the company visibly does: activity, clients, requests, follow-ups, documents, planning, team coordination.
- Frame the problem as scattered information and manual execution, not lack of tools.
- Use a clear AVANT / APRÈS structure so the recipient instantly understands the operational delta.
- Give 1–2 workflow examples tied to their business.
- Position IA as protecting human service and freeing high-value work — not replacing the team.
- Prefer a custom interactive page over a heavy PDF for first contact; use the page as appointment support, not a mandatory detour.

### Personalized projection + mini-maquette concept

For cold email + call outreach, the core asset should be a personalized “projection” page, not a brochure. The page is the container (`vividflow.co/prenom-nom` / company-specific URL); the mini-maquette inside is the visual proof of what their own AI operating infrastructure could look like.

When Thomas/Jonathan ask for the maquette itself, do **not** stay at strategy level. Produce a concrete multi-page version: exact page titles, short body copy, visual/UI components, and CTA. If they ask for variants, give 5 strong content variants per page and then state the recommended winner. They want enough detail for a designer/builder to execute, not just the logic.

The mini-maquette should make the prospect feel “this is applied to my company” and “this is not a CRM/chatbot/tool to learn.” Show:
- business memory: the AI knows the company, offers, clients, documents, processes, tone, and priorities;
- central AI right hand: one operational AI brain coordinating the system;
- specialized agents: assistants for relances, client requests, preparation, reporting, coordination, commercial follow-up, etc.;
- existing-tool layer: works over/with CRM, email, agenda, documents, WhatsApp, forms, files; avoid API jargon at first, but mention that direct connections or bridges are possible when useful;
- human operator: VividFlow designs, installs, supervises, and improves the system;
- business impact: fewer forgotten follow-ups, faster responses, team focused on revenue/value work, leader back above operations;
- ownership/security: client owns the infrastructure, data, business logic, and knowledge base; VividFlow is implementation/operator partner, not lock-in owner.

Language to prefer:
- “infrastructure IA propriétaire”
- “couche d’exécution intelligente”
- “vos données / vos agents / votre système”
- “pas un chatbot type ChatGPT/Claude, pas un CRM de plus”

### Competitive/local tension without fake bluff

Use competitor context to create personalization and urgency, not false claims. Safe framing:
- mention visible comparable actors in the same sector/canton as market context;
- emphasize advantage from faster execution, follow-up, client response, and team coordination;
- say VividFlow limits identical deployments among direct competitors when relevant, because a proprietary infrastructure should create operational advantage and not be copy-pasted.

Avoid implying VividFlow is already working with or contacting named competitors unless true. Swiss B2B trust/discretion matters more than a manipulative FOMO spike.

## Brainstorm vs script

When Jonathan says he wants to brainstorm, do not jump into scripts, plans, or execution. Treat the moment as exploration between Jonathan, Thomas, and the assistant. The user may be sending information only to be registered; do not answer with an opinion, recommendation, or action framework unless he explicitly asks for your avis, a decision, a script, or next steps.

For acquisition brainstorming with Jonathan:
- first receive and reflect the current premise in 1–3 short lines;
- ask at most one clarifying question if the idea cannot progress;
- keep the tone like an active working conversation, not a keyword-heavy strategy memo;
- do not over-explain validated angles or rename them casually once validated;
- if an angle is validated, treat it as locked and move to the next angle;
- avoid dumping scripts, objection handling, or long frameworks unless Jonathan explicitly asks;
- only write the call script once he asks for “démo”, “script”, “adapte”, “prompt”, or another execution cue;
- use simple language and short reasoning: Jonathan is testing the idea, not asking for polished copy yet.

Pitfalls from Jonathan corrections:
- Do not keep inserting scripts when the ask is still brainstorming.
- Do not over-focus on objections before the acquisition strategy and outreach concept are settled.
- Do not frame every appointment as “Jonathan will show you”; in outreach, the front may be a female team member/setter and the appointment can be with “l’équipe VividFlow” or the appropriate operator depending on setup.
- For phone scripts, avoid robotic transitions like “ok super / ok parfait” and long monologues. Jonathan wants a spontaneous, oral rhythm: short line, pause, let the prospect answer, then continue.
- Prefer “projection / maquette / système IA sur mesure” over generic “notre IA” or “une IA” when selling VividFlow’s operational system.
- If the prospect was sent a maquette, the call should say this directly and verify reception; do not make the targeting sound casual or random with “on regarde en ce moment…” unless the sentence clearly explains why they were targeted.

## First-contact assets and case studies

- For WhatsApp Channel nurturing and the “Cercle IA Privé” acquisition loop, use `references/whatsapp-channel-acquisition.md`.
- Create a warm trigger when possible: short personalized email/page showing the specific business angle.
- Use 2 short case studies in first-contact assets by default; 3–5 usually creates too much reading.
- Format case studies as short cards: Before / After / Business change.
- Use PDFs only as second-level downloadable detail; first click should usually be a simple personalized page.
- If the prospect is on the phone and interested, book immediately; do not send them away to read the asset first.

### Cold outreach — validated VividFlow frame

Validated entry angle for the call/email loop:
- Email/page context: “On a commencé une projection autour de [Entreprise].”
- Phone follow-up, when the decision-maker name is known: “Je voulais juste vérifier si [Prénom] l’avait bien reçue.”
- If a gatekeeper asks “Vous voulez parler à qui ?”, answer only the first name: “[Prénom].”
- Do not replace this with softer variants like “on a lancé un truc” or long explanations of how VividFlow researched the company. The intrigue comes from the short phrasing; the surprise belongs inside the projection.

Cold call role:
- The setter does not explain all personalization, competitors, website research, or technical depth.
- The call opens the door and verifies the projection reached the right decision-maker.
- The call must not become a mini-R1: avoid deep questions like “où ça pourrait soulager votre équipe ?” before the appointment.
- The projection/page creates the “wow” by revealing that VividFlow understood the company.
- Latest VividFlow outbound V1 preference: the appointment duration should be around 25–30 minutes when presenting the projection, not 15 minutes. Do not over-focus on the meeting duration during brainstorm unless Jonathan/Thomas brings it up.

Current call spine for Jonathan’s acquisition loop:
1. “On a commencé une projection autour de [Entreprise]. Je voulais juste vérifier si [Prénom] l’avait bien reçue.”
2. Let the prospect answer; do not steamroll.
3. “Le contexte en deux mots : …” then explain the method simply.
4. Mention the niche/type of company, not generic “entreprises suisses” when the pipeline has the sector: e.g. “agences immobilières”, “entreprises B2B”, “PME industrielles”.
5. Say that VividFlow prepared a first personalized projection from what was understood about their activity.
6. Transition to the appointment because the projection is clearer visually than on the phone.
7. Book 15–20 minutes: “Vous avez un créneau dispo demain ou après-demain ?”

Avoid in this call spine:
- “Je vais vous la montrer rapidement” if the projection was already sent; it breaks continuity.
- “Je vous la renvoie” as the default when they haven’t looked; it sounds odd and turns the call into email admin.
- “Est-ce que ça fait sens ?” too early; prospects may need the visual support before judging.
- “Notre métier derrière…” or brochure-like agency wording; use phone language simple enough for a 55-year-old owner and not cringe for a younger decision-maker.
- “Déléguer à l’IA” without context; it lacks immediate business value.
- Do not compress opener → booking too fast: after reception is confirmed, add a short context bridge and let the prospect respond.
- Avoid “ce qu’on vous a envoyé” as a cold transition when it feels stiff; prefer: “Oui, je vous explique juste le contexte en deux mots.”
- Keep the phone goal narrow: book the audit/reading call, not a mini-R1. Do not ask multiple diagnostic questions like “où ça pourrait soulager votre équipe ?” on the first call.
- If the projection was already emailed, do not say “je vais vous la montrer” or “je vous la renvoie” by default; it breaks the asset-assisted logic.
- For booking, use natural options like “Vous avez un créneau dispo demain ou après-demain ?” and 15–20 minutes, not 10–15 minutes.

Projection follow-up call detail: see `references/projection-follow-up-call-patterns.md`.

Validated outbound projection V1 decisions from Jonathan/Thomas: see `references/outbound-projection-v1-decisions.md`. Use it when recreating the email, personalized page structure, tone, and locked sequence.

Projection/page role:
- It should feel like a real first pass, not a brochure.
- It can use data gathered by VividFlow agents from the company website and public sources, but the call should not list that research.
- Standard scalable personalization for 100–200 leads/day: company, founder/decision-maker, canton/region, sector, activity summary, 2–3 likely operational frictions, and a mini-maquette of the future system.
- Avoid making the page look like a new CRM/SaaS tool. Show an operational layer around the existing team and tools.
- Do not open the projection with positioning defense like “pas un CRM / pas un chatbot / pas un outil de plus”. First show the prospect's concrete situation and pain; use infrastructure positioning later as reassurance.
- For slide/page iteration, if the visual is overloaded but conceptually right, prefer progressive motion/reveal over adding explanatory labels.

Positioning elements to include in the projection:
- not a chatbot type ChatGPT/Claude;
- not a CRM or extra tool to learn;
- a proprietary AI infrastructure owned by the client;
- business memory: knows the company, offers, processes, documents, rules;
- specialized agents assisting the team on commercial/operational tasks;
- human operator/supervision by VividFlow;
- works with existing tools where possible, without making API jargon the main message;
- business impact: fewer chronophage tasks, stronger follow-up, faster execution, team focused on work that creates cash.

## Quality Gate

Reject the angle if:
- It defaults to real estate when the prospect sector was not specified.
- It could apply unchanged to any SaaS.
- It does not mention a concrete workflow in the prospect’s business.
- It sells AI instead of solving operations.
- It sounds like a polished consultant/AI assistant instead of direct B2B phone language.
- It adds too many clicks between interest and appointment.
- It has no concrete next action.
- It sounds like generic AI consulting or uses jargon before the business pain is clear.

When building the phone flow for the email/projection loop, do not compress straight from opener to booking. Keep a short human context beat, let the prospect answer, then book fast.

Style constraints from Jonathan:

- Use 15–20 minutes, not 10–15.
- Prefer a visual-support transition over asking the prospect to fully understand on the phone: “C’est plus simple de la parcourir visuellement / ensemble plutôt que de tout expliquer au téléphone.”
- When Jonathan asks for a handoff prompt to Claude Code or another reviewer, include the full system logic: email structure, page/projection role, call script, positioning, CTA, and what feedback is expected. Do not send only the call transcript; the email/projection context is part of the conversion mechanism.

Current email/projection/call chain:
1. Email opens with the validated line “On a commencé une projection autour de [Entreprise]” and frames a first personalized case/projection, not a generic AI pitch.
2. Email explains VividFlow is looking at a niche/category of Swiss businesses like theirs where IA could reduce operational load.
3. Projection/page is the visual proof: personalized URL, company-specific first pass, 2–3 relevant operational points, AVANT/APRÈS, and examples of tasks better followed/prepared/treated/coordinated.
4. Call verifies receipt, gives “le contexte en deux mots”, then books 15–20 minutes to review the projection; it does not sell a demo or run the audit live.
- Booking CTA can be direct but natural: “Vous avez un créneau dispo demain ou après-demain ?”

Draft spine to keep iterating from:
1. Opener/reception check.
2. Prospect answers; pause.
3. “Le contexte en deux mots…”
4. Niche-specific reason: VividFlow looks at [type d’entreprise] in Switzerland where AI can remove operational load.
5. For [Entreprise], a personalized projection was prepared based on what was understood from their activity.
6. Value must be simple enough for a non-technical older leader and not stale for a younger one: better followed/prepared/treated/coordinated tasks; less operational load; no extra complicated tool.
7. Visual-support transition.
8. Book 15–20 minutes; collect phone + email; send calendar invite.

When Jonathan shares external cold-call training (e.g. Minozan), extract the operating system separately from the messaging:
- Keep the system: sourcing → appel → RDV → follow-up → tracking, R1/R2 split, strict KPIs, repeated relances.
- Do not blindly copy growth/apporteur d'affaires/revenue angles unless the niche demands it.
- For VividFlow, default the message to operational time saved: reduce repetitive tasks for the team and let the leader keep control without managing everything.
- Prefer a lightweight personalized page/maquette over a PDF as first-contact proof; it supports appointment booking and must not become a reading detour.
- Distinguish cold call/R1/R2: cold call books, R1 diagnoses, R2 demonstrates/sells.

## Common Mistakes

- Mistaking “AI is coming” for a conversion angle.
- Defaulting to real estate language when the prospect is a general Swiss TPE/PME.
- Writing for tech-curious people instead of pragmatic business owners.
- Hiding IA completely; Jonathan wants IA named directly, then immediately grounded in business tasks.
- Using unverified numbers.
- Treating audit/demo CTA as automatic instead of matching the current offer.
- Producing scripts when Jonathan asked for brainstorming.
